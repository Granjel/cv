# Part B

I investigate how interactions among species determine biodiversity, coexistence and ecosystem stability under environmental change. I combine field experiments with statistical and mathematical models to explain which species persist together and how communities recover after disturbance.

**Scientific contributions and initiative:** During my FPU-funded PhD at the University of Sevilla, I linked nitrogen enrichment and fungal pathogens to the mechanisms of multispecies coexistence. With Eric Allan in Bern, I showed that the number of interacting species changes the importance of direct and indirect effects on coexistence (*New Phytologist*, 2023). I co-designed the research, conducted fieldwork, analysed the data and wrote the manuscript. In *Oikos* (2026), I led the analyses and software development linking simple plant interaction patterns to structural niche and fitness differences in a multitrophic grassland community. My experimental work in chemical ecology showed that volatile signalling reduces herbivore damage in *Arabidopsis* populations with contrasting life histories (*Environmental and Experimental Botany*, 2026). I am **first and corresponding author of these three papers**. For the latter research, I conceived and led CoVOCom, securing an AEET early-career project grant and coordinating the experiments.

**Sustained international collaboration:** My research visits and fieldwork span six countries outside Spain. Repeated visits to Bern supported my grassland research and a continuing collaboration, including a first-author manuscript on land-use intensification and coexistence, currently under review. Following my stay with Tadeu Siqueira at UNESP, Brazil, I continue to collaborate with Brazilian researchers on agrochemical effects on planktonic food webs, with a manuscript in preparation. As a Fulbright fellow at North Carolina State University, USA, I conceived and conducted a project on microbial interactions, stability and functioning, hosted by William Petry. Our collaboration continues through a manuscript in preparation testing predictions of competitive outcomes. Earlier fieldwork at the Swedish University of Agricultural Sciences broadened my experience of above- and below-ground responses to disturbance. These relationships allow me to test ecological ideas across systems and sustain joint research beyond individual visits.

**Current research:** Since 2023, I have worked with Daniel Montoya at BC3 within the ERC project RECODYN. Alongside fieldwork at CNRS SETE’s terrestrial Metatron (Moulis, France), I am developing Bayesian state-space models to infer plant growth and interactions from biomass time series, accounting for observation and process uncertainty. With Jürg Spaak, whom I visited at RPTU, Germany, in 2026, I connect these estimates to coexistence theory and assembly graphs to investigate recovery pathways under warming. I also contribute experiments and field sampling to ChEMISt, linking chemical ecology with multitrophic interactions. I contributed analyses, interpretation and writing to a study distinguishing local and spatial contributions of plant diversity and functional traits to productivity stability across 235 grasslands (*Nature Communications*, 2026). Current collaborative work includes a manuscript on warming and recovery under review and another on intraspecific trait variation and stability in preparation. Together, these studies extend my coexistence research towards understanding how functional differences influence ecosystem recovery.

**Open science, training and service:** I share data and R code through public repositories, including the PaNDiv archive and software for interaction estimation and structural coexistence analyses. I have taught 192 hours at undergraduate and master’s level, co-supervised an MSc thesis and mentored two DAAD RISE Worldwide internships, one including BSc co-supervision. I serve on the PCI Ecology recommenders panel, have evaluated Fulbright fellowships and AEET projects, and served on the scientific committee and coordinated a session at the 2025 SIBECOL–AEET meeting. Through the European Researchers’ Night and school workshops, I share ecological research with wider audiences.

**Next research step:** Working with Carlos Pérez Carmona, I aim to combine my experimental and interaction-modelling experience with his expertise in functional ecology to test when functional variation predicts coexistence and recovery under environmental change.

# C.4

I contribute to open and reproducible ecology by sharing research data and analytical code in public repositories. My contributions include an MIT-licensed software release for estimating species interactions using lasso regularisation and calculating structural niche and fitness differences (2026; [doi: 10.5281/zenodo.23054165](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23054165)), and a PaNDiv data-and-code archive released under CC BY 4.0 (2022; [doi: 10.5281/zenodo.7343389](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.7343389)). By making these analytical workflows available alongside the underlying data, I help other researchers reproduce the analyses and adapt the methods to their own ecological questions.

I have also participated in public science outreach through the European Researchers’ Night and school workshops, sharing ecological research with non-specialist and younger audiences. These activities complement the dissemination of my research within the scientific community.
