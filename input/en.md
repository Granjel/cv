# Part B

I study how species interactions shape coexistence, ecosystem functioning and recovery under environmental change. I combine coexistence theory and ecological network approaches with experiments and quantitative models. My MSc research linked soil organisms and environmental conditions to carbon exports from peatlands (*Geoderma*, 2020).

During my **FPU-funded PhD**, I investigated how environmental change affects coexistence beyond pairs of species. With Eric Allan in Bern, I co-designed experiments, analysed data and wrote a paper showing that nutrient and pathogen effects depend on the number of interacting species (*New Phytologist*, 2023). During five months of visits supported by the University of Bern, I helped maintain the PaNDiv experiment. This collaboration continues with a manuscript on land-use intensification and coexistence, under review. In *Oikos* (2026), I led analyses, software development and writing to link simple plant interaction patterns to coexistence in a complex plant–herbivore community.

In parallel, I developed a chemical-ecology collaboration outside my doctoral group through visits to MBG-CSIC, including one funded by a competitive FPU mobility grant. I conceived and led the **AEET-funded CoVOCom project** to investigate how chemical communication affects plant responses to herbivory. Its experiments showed that volatile signalling reduces damage in *Arabidopsis* (*Environmental and Experimental Botany*, 2026). I am **first and corresponding author of these three papers**. Work at MBG also produced a *Plant, Cell & Environment* paper (2021) from an LMU Munich BSc thesis I co-supervised.

Further collaborations extended my research to aquatic and microbial communities. A five-month Santander-funded stay with Tadeu Siqueira at UNESP (Rio Claro, Brazil) developed into continuing research with UNESP and UFSCar on plankton coexistence under agrochemical stress; I am **last and corresponding author** of the manuscript in preparation. During a **ten-month Fulbright fellowship** with William Petry at North Carolina State University, I established protist experiments for a project I conceived on microbial interactions, stability and functioning. Two manuscripts nearing submission test predictions of pairwise competition and multispecies coexistence against experimental data. The laboratory and Bayesian modelling skills I developed there now inform my postdoctoral research.

At BC3, with Daniel Montoya within ERC Consolidator RECODYN, I now ask **which routes of community recovery are possible under warming**. I develop Bayesian models to infer plant growth and interactions from experimental biomass data and, with Jürg Spaak at RPTU (Germany), connect these estimates to assembly graphs describing possible sequences of species establishment and community endpoints. I established this collaboration to link empirical recovery dynamics with coexistence theory, carrying uncertainty in interaction estimates into recovery predictions. Research visits to CNRS SETE (France; 173 days in 2023–2025) supported joint work with local teams using the Metatron facility. Through AgroServ’s Horizon Europe access programme, ChEMISt connects this work with my chemical-ecology research using the same infrastructure. I am first author of two postdoctoral manuscripts in progress. I also contributed analyses, interpretation and writing to a study of functional diversity and stability across 235 grasslands (*Nature Communications*, 2026).

For **every published paper I have led**, I have archived well-documented data and reproducible analysis code in permanent public repositories, including Zenodo and Dryad. I have taught 192 hours at undergraduate and master’s level, co-supervised an MSc thesis and mentored two DAAD RISE Worldwide interns at MBG-CSIC. I serve on the PCI Ecology recommenders panel, have evaluated Fulbright fellowships and AEET projects, and served on the scientific committee and coordinated a session at SIBECOL–AEET 2025. I organised my group’s contribution to the European Researchers’ Night, bringing together BC3 and EHU researchers to explain biodiversity and human pressures through games and visual activities for children and adults.

With Carlos Pérez Carmona, I aim to connect this experimental and modelling background with functional ecology to test how functional differences influence coexistence and recovery under environmental change.
