# Part B

**Scientific profile and expertise:** I am a community ecologist studying how interactions among species and environmental conditions shape biodiversity, ecosystem stability and functioning. I combine experiments with statistical and mathematical approaches to connect observations with ecological theory. My research spans plant communities, plant–herbivore interactions, chemical communication, microbial communities and soil ecology. Across these systems, I ask how coexistence mechanisms arise, how they respond to global change, and what they reveal about the persistence and recovery of ecological communities. I am particularly interested in the relationship between the interaction strengths estimated from data and the mechanisms that permit species to persist. This requires comparing alternative explanations, accounting for uncertainty and being explicit about the scale at which conclusions apply. Rather than treating community patterns as isolated outcomes, I aim to relate them to testable hypotheses about environmental responses, feedbacks and the organisation of ecological interactions.

**Research contributions:** During my PhD in Integrative Biology at the University of Sevilla, I investigated how nutrient availability and interactions among multiple species affect plant coexistence. My work links nitrogen enrichment and foliar fungal pathogens to structural niche and fitness differences, considering how the number of interacting species changes the importance of direct and indirect effects. I have also studied interaction networks in a multitrophic grassland community, using lasso regularisation and structural stability theory to relate patterns of plant interactions to coexistence mechanisms. These studies connect experimentally measured responses with quantities that help explain community composition.

**Complementary research:** My work in chemical ecology examines how interactions between plants and their enemies affect communication and resistance. I have investigated volatile-mediated signalling in Arabidopsis thaliana populations with contrasting life histories and contributed to experiments on fungal infection and airborne communication between potato plants. I also collaborate on questions linking plant diversity to the stability of grassland productivity across spatial scales. Earlier research on peatlands broadened my interests in below-ground communities and carbon cycling. Together, these projects provide complementary ways to study how interactions connect biodiversity with ecosystem processes.

**Current programme and international experience:** Since May 2023, I have worked as a postdoctoral researcher at the Basque Centre for Climate Change. Within RECODYN, I contribute to experiments, field sampling, data analysis and Bayesian state-space modelling of species interactions, coexistence and community recovery under warming. Through ChEMISt, I participate in experimental and field work connecting phytochemistry, functional traits and multitrophic interactions. My Fulbright research fellowship at North Carolina State University allowed me to investigate the role of interactions in microbial community stability and functioning, hosted by William K. Petry. These experiences have expanded the range of systems and analytical approaches that inform my research.

**Research leadership and scientific service:** I conceived and led CoVOCom, obtaining AEET funding to investigate coexistence and chemical communication between plants in response to herbivory. I have also contributed as a researcher to collaborative projects led by other investigators, developing experience in both individual and team-based research. My service includes manuscript reviewing, participation in the PCI Ecology recommenders panel, and evaluation of Fulbright predoctoral fellowships and AEET research proposals. I have contributed to scientific meetings through oral presentations, posters and invited seminars, as well as scientific committee work, session coordination and moderation at the 2025 SIBECOL–AEET meeting.

**Training, openness and engagement:** I have taught undergraduate courses at the University of Sevilla and contributed to postgraduate teaching at the University of the Basque Country. My training activities include co-supervision of an MSc thesis and tutoring research internships through the DAAD RISE Worldwide programme. I use R for statistical analysis, modelling and visualisation, together with version control and reproducible workflows. I share data and analytical code through public repositories, including resources for multispecies coexistence and species-interaction analyses, to make the methods and results accessible for scrutiny and reuse. I have also participated in the European Researchers’ Night and school workshops, communicating ecological research to non-specialist audiences. My future research will continue to connect experimental observations, interaction models and biodiversity responses to environmental change.

# C.4

I contribute to open and reproducible ecology by sharing research data and analytical code in public repositories. My contributions include an MIT-licensed software release for estimating species interactions using lasso regularisation and calculating structural niche and fitness differences (2026; [doi: 10.5281/zenodo.23054165](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23054165)), and a PaNDiv data-and-code archive released under CC BY 4.0 (2022; [doi: 10.5281/zenodo.7343389](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.7343389)). By making these analytical workflows available alongside the underlying data, I help other researchers reproduce the analyses and adapt the methods to their own ecological questions.

I have also participated in public science outreach through the European Researchers’ Night and school workshops, sharing ecological research with non-specialist and younger audiences. These activities complement the dissemination of my research within the scientific community.
