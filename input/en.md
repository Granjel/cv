# Part B

I study how species interactions shape the maintenance and recovery of ecosystem stability and functioning under environmental change, combining coexistence theory and ecological networks with experiments and quantitative models.

A recurring problem in my work is how to explain whole communities from the interactions among their species. My FPU-funded doctoral research addressed this through plant coexistence under nutrient enrichment, pathogens and herbivory. Research with Eric Allan’s group in Bern (approximately 20 weeks, supported by the host) developed my experience in community experiments and quantitative coexistence theory. I led data collection, analyses and writing for a study showing that nutrient and pathogen effects on coexistence depend on how many species interact (*New Phytologist*, 2023). I also helped maintain PaNDiv; our collaboration continues with a manuscript on land-use intensification and coexistence, under review. In *Oikos* (2026), I developed analyses and software linking interaction patterns in a multitrophic grassland community to coexistence mechanisms, and showed that plant–plant interactions outweighed herbivory effects on plant dynamics.

My work on chemical communication examined how plants modify their interactions with neighbours and herbivores. I **conceived CoVOCom**, obtained competitive AEET funding and led the project to study plant chemical communication under herbivory. Through approximately eight months at MBG-CSIC, outside my doctoral group and partly supported by FPU mobility funding, I developed experiments showing that volatile communication increases herbivory resistance in two contrasting *Arabidopsis thaliana* populations (*Environmental and Experimental Botany*, 2026). I am **first and corresponding author** of these three papers. At MBG, co-supervision of an LMU Munich BSc thesis also led to a *Plant, Cell & Environment* paper (2021).

Microbial systems expanded my ability to test how interactions determine coexistence and functioning. During an approximately ten-month **Fulbright fellowship** with William Petry at North Carolina State University, I established protist experiments for a project I conceived and led. Two joint manuscripts nearing submission test predictions of pairwise competition and multispecies coexistence. The laboratory and Bayesian modelling skills I acquired now underpin my postdoctoral work. A collaboration begun during a Santander-funded stay at UNESP, Brazil (approximately five months), continues with UNESP and UFSCar on plankton coexistence under agrochemical stress; I contribute to conceptual development, supervision and writing as last and corresponding author of a manuscript in preparation.

My postdoctoral research at BC3, within Daniel Montoya’s ERC Consolidator RECODYN project, extends these questions from community persistence to **recovery after disturbance**. I develop Bayesian models to infer plant growth and interactions and investigate which recovery routes and final communities are possible under warming. Research stays at CNRS SETE, France (173 days in 2023–2025), involved sustained work with local teams on experiments using Metatron. I established a collaboration with Jürg Spaak at RPTU, Germany (approximately two months), to connect these interaction estimates to community assembly and recovery predictions while accounting for uncertainty. Two first-author postdoctoral manuscripts are in preparation. Through ChEMISt (AgroServ/Horizon Europe), complementary experiments and fieldwork are helping me connect chemical ecology with this recovery programme.

Explaining why interactions vary between species and environments is a developing direction in this work. I contributed analyses, interpretation and writing to a study of 235 grasslands (*Nature Communications*, 2026), examining how trait effects on stability depend on spatial scale. With Carlos Pérez Carmona, I aim to connect this trait perspective with interaction mechanisms and test its implications for coexistence and recovery.

I share documented data and reproducible code through permanent repositories, including Zenodo and Dryad, for every published paper I have led. I have taught 192 hours, co-supervised an MSc thesis and mentored two DAAD RISE Worldwide interns. I serve on the PCI Ecology recommenders panel, have evaluated Fulbright fellowships and AEET projects, and coordinated a scientific session at SIBECOL–AEET 2025. I also organised my group’s European Researchers’ Night activity with BC3 and EHU colleagues, using games and visual activities to explain biodiversity and human pressures to children and adults.
