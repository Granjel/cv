# Part B

I study how species interactions shape coexistence, ecosystem functioning and recovery under environmental change, combining coexistence theory and ecological network approaches with experiments and quantitative models. I am **first and corresponding author of three research papers**: *New Phytologist* (2023), *Environmental and Experimental Botany* (2026) and *Oikos* (2026).

During my FPU-funded PhD, I led data collection, analyses and manuscript preparation for the *New Phytologist* study with Eric Allan at the University of Bern. We showed that nutrient and pathogen effects on coexistence depend on the number of interacting species. Across five months of visits supported by Bern, I also helped maintain PaNDiv and gave an invited seminar. This collaboration continues with a manuscript on land-use intensification and coexistence, under review. In *Oikos*, I led analyses, software development and writing, showing that plant–plant interactions outweighed herbivory effects on plant dynamics and linking interaction patterns to coexistence mechanisms.

I conceived **CoVOCom**, obtained competitive AEET funding and led the project as PI to study chemical communication and herbivory. I developed this research through stays at MBG-CSIC, outside my doctoral group, including one supported by a competitive FPU mobility grant. The resulting *Environmental and Experimental Botany* paper showed that volatile communication increases herbivory resistance in two contrasting *Arabidopsis thaliana* populations. At MBG, I also co-supervised an LMU Munich BSc thesis that led to a *Plant, Cell & Environment* paper (2021).

A **five-month Santander-funded stay at UNESP**, Rio Claro, Brazil, developed into continuing research with UNESP and UFSCar on plankton coexistence under agrochemical stress; I am last and corresponding author of the manuscript in preparation. During a **ten-month Fulbright fellowship** with William Petry at North Carolina State University, I established protist experiments for a project I conceived and led on microbial interactions, stability and functioning. Two joint manuscripts nearing submission test predictions of pairwise competition and multispecies coexistence. The laboratory and Bayesian modelling skills acquired there now underpin my postdoctoral work.

At BC3, within Daniel Montoya’s ERC Consolidator RECODYN project, I investigate which recovery routes and community endpoints are possible under warming. I develop Bayesian models to infer plant growth and interactions from experimental biomass data. I established a collaboration with Jürg Spaak at RPTU (Germany), where I undertook a research stay and gave invited seminars, to connect these estimates to assembly graphs and propagate uncertainty in interactions into recovery predictions. During research stays at CNRS SETE, France (173 days in 2023–2025), I worked with local teams on experiments using Metatron. Through ChEMISt (AgroServ/Horizon Europe), I also contribute experiments and fieldwork that connect my chemical-ecology and recovery research. Two first-author postdoctoral manuscripts are in progress. I also contributed statistical analyses, interpretation and writing to a study across 235 grasslands showing that the role of functional traits in ecosystem stability depends on spatial scale (*Nature Communications*, 2026).

For **every published paper I have led**, I have deposited documented data and reproducible analysis code in permanent public repositories, including Zenodo and Dryad. I have taught 192 hours at undergraduate and master’s level, co-supervised an MSc thesis and mentored two DAAD RISE Worldwide interns at MBG-CSIC. I serve on the PCI Ecology recommenders panel, have evaluated Fulbright fellowships and AEET projects, and served on the scientific committee and coordinated a session at SIBECOL–AEET 2025. I organised my group’s Researchers’ Night activity with BC3 and EHU colleagues, explaining biodiversity and human pressures through games and visual activities for children and adults.

With Carlos Pérez Carmona, I aim to combine this experimental and modelling experience with functional ecology to test how functional differences influence coexistence and recovery under environmental change.
