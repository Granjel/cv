# Part B

I investigate how species interactions determine which species can persist together under environmental change. I combine detailed empirical data from my own field and laboratory experiments and international collaborations with mathematical models grounded in coexistence theory and ecological network approaches. My work lies at the intersection of experimental and quantitative ecology.

**Scientific contributions and initiative:** During my FPU-funded PhD at the University of Sevilla, I co-designed experiments, analysed data and wrote a paper showing that nutrient and pathogen effects on coexistence depend on the number of interacting species (*New Phytologist*, 2023). In *Oikos* (2026), I led analyses and software development showing how simple plant interactions help explain coexistence within a complex food web. I conceived and led the AEET-funded CoVOCom project, whose experiments showed that volatile signalling reduces herbivore damage in *Arabidopsis* (*Environmental and Experimental Botany*, 2026). I am **first and corresponding author of these three papers**. Visits to MBG-CSIC, including one funded by a competitive FPU mobility grant, helped me develop this research line and also led to a paper in *Plant, Cell & Environment* (2021).

**Sustained international collaboration:** Across five months of visits to Eric Allan’s group at the University of Bern, supported by the university, I helped maintain the PaNDiv experiment and gave a seminar. This collaboration continues through a first-author manuscript on land-use intensification and coexistence, under review. A five-month Santander-funded stay with Tadeu Siqueira at UNESP (Rio Claro, Brazil), including a short visit to UFSCar, initiated a collaboration on agrochemical effects on planktonic food webs. I am **last and corresponding author** of the manuscript in preparation. Earlier visits to SLU (Sweden) broadened my experience of responses of plants and soil organisms to disturbance.

**Methods acquired abroad:** During a ten-month Fulbright fellowship with William Petry at North Carolina State University, I established protist experiments to study microbial interactions, stability and functioning, and acquired skills in laboratory work and Bayesian modelling. Two joint manuscripts are nearing submission. I now apply these Bayesian methods in my postdoctoral research.

**Postdoctoral research:** At BC3, I work with Daniel Montoya within the ERC Consolidator project RECODYN, developing Bayesian state-space models that account for observation error when estimating plant interactions and community recovery under warming. Research visits to CNRS SETE (Moulis, France; 173 days in 2023–2025) enabled joint work with local research teams using the Metatron facility. I established a collaboration with Jürg Spaak at RPTU (Germany), where I gave a seminar, to connect these models with coexistence theory and assembly graphs. Through AgroServ’s Horizon Europe access programme, I also contribute experiments and fieldwork to ChEMISt, connecting my research on coexistence and chemical ecology using the same infrastructure. I contributed analyses, interpretation and writing to a study of functional diversity and stability across 235 grasslands (*Nature Communications*, 2026). This postdoctoral work includes two first-author manuscripts in progress.

**Open science, training and service:** I share data and R code in public repositories, including the PaNDiv archive and software for estimating interactions and analysing coexistence. I have taught 192 hours at undergraduate and master’s level, co-supervised an MSc thesis, and mentored two DAAD RISE Worldwide interns at MBG-CSIC, including co-supervision of an LMU Munich BSc thesis. I serve on the PCI Ecology recommenders panel, have evaluated Fulbright fellowships and AEET projects, and served on the scientific committee and coordinated a session at SIBECOL–AEET 2025. I participate in the European Researchers’ Night and school workshops. I also provided a biological expert assessment for judicial proceedings (2019).

**Next research step:** With Carlos Pérez Carmona, I aim to combine my experimental experience and skills in modelling interactions with his expertise in functional ecology to test when functional variation predicts coexistence and recovery under environmental change.
